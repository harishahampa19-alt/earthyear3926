The calendar, the week and the Moon
never realign.

Start from 30 June 2026, 00:00 UT — a Tuesday, at a full moon. Go forward a whole number of calendar years. When does the terminal date land on a Tuesday and at a full moon again?

Theorem — the solution set is empty

C1 forces D = 7Z. C3 taken exactly forces D = K·M. Writing M as the rational 295305889 / 107 makes 7·Z·107 = K·295305889 an integer equation. The numerator is odd, is not a multiple of 5, and leaves remainder 4 modulo 7 — so it is coprime to both 7 and 107. Therefore 7·107 divides K.

The first exact solution needs lunations — about tropical years. Nothing below that works, and that one is an artifact of writing M to seven decimals. For the real synodic month, which is irrational and drifting, the set is empty outright.

And yet inf |error| = 0. Each 400-year block advances the phase by 146097 days, which is not a whole number of lunations, so the near-solutions keep improving without bound. This project maps them.

  • C1 Z(Y) = D(Y) / 7 ∈ ℤ Same weekday at both ends. An exact integer test — no ephemeris needed.
  • C2 D(Y) = 365Y + L(Y) Whole calendar years, Gregorian leap rule. Non-selective; the residual against Y·365.2421897 is recorded, never used to filter.
  • C3 slip = D − K·M ≈ 0 Whole lunations, with K = round(D / 29.5305889).
  • C4 E(Y) = minh |θ − 180°| < ε Actual alignment at the terminal date, from DE441 over the 24 UT hours of that day.

The founding hypothesis, and why it failed

This project began with a pair of dates: 30 June 1987 and 30 June 2026. Both Tuesdays, exactly 39 calendar years and 2035 whole weeks apart. The expectation was that the Moon would come back too.

The cause

39 = 19 + 19 + 1. The Metonic cycle of 19 years is lunations — very nearly whole, which is why 38 years preserves lunar phase to of a lunation. The extra single year adds lunations. That leftover of a cycle is days of slip, and it is the whole story: the calendar and the week closed, the Moon did not.

The negative result is part of the finding. C1 and C2 are cheap to satisfy — years in the scan do it. Satisfying C3 and C4 as well is what is impossible, and the 1987 pair is the cleanest demonstration of the gap between the two.

Methods, and what these numbers do not mean

Ephemeris

, JPL DE441. Segments used here are valid . Positions are geocentric apparent ecliptic longitudes in the true ecliptic and equinox of date, light-time corrected, via Skyfield .

θ is verified identical to skyfield.almanac.moon_phase to within 10−9 degrees by the test suite.

Why not mean elements

A mean-element lunar theory drifts by degrees within a thousand years of the epoch and by tens of degrees within five thousand. The effects measured here are sub-degree, so an approximation of that class would be larger than the entire signal. Nothing on this page uses one.

T_trop and M_syn appear only as bookkeeping: K, slip and the tropical residual. Never as a source of position.

The hourly grid

E is a minimum over the 24 whole UT hours of the terminal date, as defined. θ moves about 0.508° per hour, so the grid alone contributes up to ±0.254°. An E below roughly a quarter of a degree therefore says the full moon fell close to a whole hour, not only that it fell on that day.

slip is the sampling-free companion measure: it is continuous, and its smallest absolute value in the scan is days.

ΔT in the far future

Converting UT to dynamical time needs ΔT, which is extrapolated beyond the observed record. At the far end of this scan the model value is about hours, with an uncertainty plausibly of the same order.

Since θ moves 0.508° per hour, E values past roughly the year 2500 are reproducible but not predictive. They are exact statements about a defined time scale, not forecasts of what a future observer would see.

Column accuracy

  • Y, D, L, Z, K — exact integers.
  • slip — exact to double precision, from the defining constant.
  • E, θ — ephemeris-limited to well under 0.001°, then grid-limited as above.
  • lit — true phase angle, not the (1 − cos θ)/2 proxy.
  • digital roots — exact integers; presented as curiosities, with no claim attached.

Two published values that fail

The founding write-up lists Y = 1031 at ≈179.98° and Y = 4374 at ≈180.000°. Neither reproduces.

  • Y = 1031 passes C1, but θ measures — about 105° from full.
  • Y = 4374 fails C1 outright: D = 1597571, which is 3 mod 7, so the ends are different weekdays.

Both are kept as regression tests rather than removed.
